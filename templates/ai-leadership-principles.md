# AI Leadership Principles

Last reviewed: 2026-07-24. See [sources and verification notes](../sources.md).

Use these as a short leadership statement before detailed policy. Adapt to your organisation, sector, jurisdiction, and risk appetite.

## Principle 1: AI Serves a Named Outcome

We do not adopt AI to appear current. Every material AI initiative must name the operational, customer, staff, quality, risk, or strategic outcome it serves.

## Principle 2: Human Accountability Remains

AI may draft, suggest, summarise, compare, and accelerate. A named human remains accountable for decisions, advice, customer commitments, employee decisions, public output, and material work.

## Principle 3: Data Rules Come First

Public tools are not used for client, customer, employee, personal, confidential, financial, legal, health, safeguarding, credential, proprietary, or regulated data. Approved tools still need owners, settings, data limits, and review.

## Principle 4: Learn Before You Scale

We start with low-risk, useful workflows. We test, measure, and learn before moving AI into customer-facing, decision-shaping, regulated, or high-risk uses.

## Principle 5: Literacy Is Role-Based

AI literacy is not one generic course. People need training proportionate to their role, tools, data, decisions, and the people affected by their work.

## Principle 6: Value Means Better Use of Time

Saved time must be intentionally redirected to higher-value work, better service, quality, learning, resilience, or rest. More output is not automatically more value.

## Principle 7: Risks Are Made Visible

Incidents, near misses, tool changes, vendor changes, and policy exceptions are reported and reviewed. Silence is not assurance.
