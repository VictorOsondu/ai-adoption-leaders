Brief leaders on:

- approved tools and data boundaries
- workflows that are encouraged, optional, restricted, or prohibited
- how to discuss AI in team meetings and one-to-ones
- where questions, tool requests, concerns, and incidents go
- how to recognise learning without rewarding output volume or unsafe speed

## 3. Team Rhythm: What

Managers and senior individual contributors should:

- demonstrate specific, approved workflows from their own work
- share corrections, failed attempts, and limits as well as wins
- make time for questions without assuming silence means confidence
- identify repeated work worth testing through a controlled pilot
- escalate workforce, data, customer, or safety concerns
- feed useful examples into the approved use-case library

## 6. Communication Check

- [ ] The outcome is clearer than "keep up with AI."
- [ ] Messages distinguish permission to learn from pressure to use AI everywhere.
- [ ] Leaders know how to translate direction for their function.
- [ ] People can find policy, approved tools, training, and help.
- [ ] Questions and credible concerns have a response route.
- [ ] Follow-up communication is scheduled.
- [ ] Leadership behaviour matches the stated guardrails.
