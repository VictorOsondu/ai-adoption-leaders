# Board Briefing and Adoption Metrics

Two tools for the people who fund and govern AI adoption: a one-page board briefing template, and a short set of metrics that measure value, risk, and decisions rather than activity.

Last reviewed: 2026-07-24. See [sources and verification notes](../sources.md).

---

## Part 1 — One-Page Board Briefing

Fill the brackets. Keep the board paper short; keep the evidence in the supporting registers and logs.

**AI Adoption — Board Briefing**

*Prepared by [name/role] · Date [date] · Review [quarterly]*

**1. Why we're doing this.**
[The real outcome — e.g. "recover staff time for client-facing work" / "reduce turnaround on X" — not "keep up with AI".]

**2. Where we are.**
Organisation stage: **[Nomad / Observer / Dabbler / Integrator / Architect / Innovator]**. Workforce is broadly at [stage]; leadership at [stage]. The gap we're managing: [brief].

**3. What changed this quarter.**
[Policy adopted · pilot run · training delivered · champions named · tool approved · high-risk use escalated — 3-5 bullets.]

**4. What value it returned.**
[Time saved estimate · cycle-time change · quality improvement · where saved time went · one concrete example.]

**5. Risk and controls.**
[Risk appetite · approved tools · high-risk use cases · incidents/near misses · data exposure · vendor/model changes · overdue reviews · AI literacy evidence where applicable.]

**6. Decisions needed.**
[Approve / pause / fund / escalate / retire / change risk appetite / resource champions / require further review.]

## Part 2 — Metrics That Matter

Track a few leading and lagging indicators. Avoid a dashboard nobody reads.

### Leading Indicators

- **Training coverage by role** — % of relevant staff with real AI-literacy training, not just a link.
- **Approved-tool use** — % using approved tools for real work in the last month.
- **Champion reach** — teams with a named, active champion vs teams without.
- **Question volume** — questions to the blame-free channel. Rising is healthy early on; silence often means fear, not competence.
- **Tool-register coverage** — known AI tools mapped against owner, use case, data class, and review date.
- **High-risk use review** — proposed high-risk uses reviewed before deployment.

### Lagging Indicators

- **Time recovered** — estimated hours/week saved in selected workflows. A rough, honest number beats a precise fake one.
- **Cycle time** — turnaround on a chosen recurring task, before vs after.
- **Quality outcome** — rework, error rate, customer response, staff satisfaction, or other relevant quality measure.
- **Incidents and near misses** — data-rule breaches, published errors, unsafe outputs, prompt-injection issues, customer complaints. Target: caught by review, not by a client.
- **Overdue reviews** — expired tool, risk, or policy reviews.

### The One Metric Everyone Skips

- **Where the saved time went.** For the hours AI recovered this quarter, did they go to higher-value work, customer time, better quality, learning, resilience, or rest? Or did they refill with more low-value output? If you can't answer, you're measuring speed, not value.

### Vanity Metrics to Ignore

- Number of prompts run.
- Number of logins.
- Number of AI-touched documents.
- Number of tools trialled.
- Training attendance with no behaviour or risk evidence.

Activity isn't adoption, and it isn't value.

For optional definitions of reach and repeat-use signals, use the [AI Adoption Measurement Framework](https://github.com/VictorOsondu/ai-adoption-toolkit/blob/main/measurement/adoption-measurement-framework.md). Report aggregated signals and do not rank employees by logins, prompts, or AI-generated output.
