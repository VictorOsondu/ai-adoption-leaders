# Board Briefing & Adoption Metrics

Two tools for the people who fund and govern AI adoption: a one-page board briefing template, and a short set of metrics that measure what actually matters — not vanity numbers.

---

## Part 1 — One-page board briefing

Fill the brackets. Keep it to one page; a board that wants more will ask.

**AI Adoption — Board Briefing**

*Prepared by [name/role] · Date [date] · Review [quarterly]*

**1. Why we're doing this (one sentence).**
[The real outcome — e.g. "recover staff time for client-facing work" / "reduce turnaround on X" — not "keep up with AI".]

**2. Where we are.**
Organisation stage: **[Nomad / Observer / Dabbler / Integrator / Architect / Innovator]**. Workforce is broadly at [stage]; leadership at [stage]. The gap we're managing: [brief].

**3. What we've done this quarter.**
[Policy adopted · pilot run · training delivered · champions named — 3–4 bullets.]

**4. What it's returned.**
[Time saved (estimate) · where that time went · one concrete example. Be honest about the accountability question below.]

**5. Risk position.**
[Policy in place? Data rules understood? Any incidents? Regulated-market exposure — e.g. EU AI Act Article 4 literacy obligation — covered?]

**6. What we're asking for.**
[Decision, budget, or endorsement needed — specific.]

## Part 2 — Metrics that matter

Track a few leading indicators, not a dashboard nobody reads.

### Leading indicators (predict adoption)

- **Coverage** — % of staff who've had real AI-literacy training (not "sent a link").
- **Active use** — % using an approved tool for real work in the last month.
- **Champion reach** — teams with a named, active champion vs teams without.
- **Question volume** — questions to the blame-free channel. *Rising is healthy* early on; silence often means fear, not competence.

### Lagging indicators (confirm value)

- **Time recovered** — estimated hours/week saved in the functions using AI. A rough, honest number beats a precise fake one.
- **Cycle time** — turnaround on a chosen recurring task, before vs after.
- **Incidents** — data-rule breaches, published errors. Target: caught by review, not by a client.

### The one metric everyone skips

- **Where the saved time went.** For the hours AI recovered this quarter — did they go to higher-value work, customer time, or rest? Or did they refill with more low-value output? If you can't answer, you're measuring speed, not value.

### Vanity metrics to ignore

- Number of prompts run, logins, or "AI-touched" documents. Activity isn't adoption, and it isn't value.

*Adapt to your governance cadence. In regulated or client-serving organisations, align the risk section with your DPO and the EU AI Act Article 4 literacy obligation. Not legal advice.*
