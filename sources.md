# Sources and Verification Notes

Last reviewed: 2026-07-24.

This playbook is leadership and governance guidance, not legal, data-protection, employment, insurance, regulatory, or board-duty advice. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, sector, client contract, tool, account type, data class, and use case.

The GitHub AI Adoption Playbook by GitHub is used and adapted with changes under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Attribution does not imply GitHub endorsement of this playbook.

## AI Literacy and Governance

- [GitHub: How to build an AI-powered workforce](https://github.com/github/ai-adoption-playbook) - first-party practitioner account of GitHub's internal AI enablement model. Used here as a source of operating patterns, not as independent proof that every practice transfers to every organisation.
- [GitHub: Executive support](https://github.com/github/ai-adoption-playbook/blob/main/pillar_docs/exec_support.md) - describes a communication cascade from executive direction through functional translation to front-line practice, plus visible leader learning.
- [GitHub: Why You Need a DRI for Your AI Program](https://github.com/github/ai-adoption-playbook/blob/main/pillar_docs/dri.md) - describes a dedicated programme lead connecting strategy, support, communities, tooling, policy, and measurement.
- [European Commission: AI literacy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-talent-skills-and-literacy) - Article 4 of the EU AI Act entered into application on 2025-02-02 and requires providers and deployers to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy for staff and other people dealing with AI systems on their behalf.
- [European Commission: AI literacy Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers) - explains Article 4 scope, context, affected persons, enforcement, and the Commission's approach.
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) - voluntary framework for managing AI risks to individuals, organisations, and society.
- [NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) - describes Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage functions, with governance as continuous and cross-cutting.
- [OECD AI Principles](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/ai-principles.html) - intergovernmental principles for trustworthy AI, including human-centred values, transparency, robustness, security, safety, and accountability.
- [ISO/IEC 42001 overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) - AI management-system standard. Useful for formal governance design, but not a generic compliance guarantee.

## Claims to Re-check Often

- EU AI Act Article 4 implementation and national enforcement guidance.
- Whether an organisation is acting as provider, deployer, importer, distributor, or product manufacturer for a given AI system.
- Tool-level training, retention, deletion, human-review, memory, connected-app, export, and admin controls.
- Sector-specific obligations for employment, finance, legal, health, education, public-sector, consumer, or safety-critical uses.
- Whether board reporting reflects real value, risk, incidents, and decisions rather than activity metrics.
